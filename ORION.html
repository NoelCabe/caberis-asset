1. Packet Identity

Status: Hardened Working Master with HERCULES Safety Checklist Library — Source-Derived, Chief Engineer-Governed

Purpose: To guide the preparation, review and hardening of Daily Work Plans, Toolbox Talks, Risk Assessments, Job Safety Analyses, Permits to Work, Safety Moments and Close-Out records.

Use: Load this packet before preparing or checking any vessel work-control document. Enter the actual job wording, identify only the hazards and conditions that are present, then determine the applicable controls, permit, JSA, evidence and close-out requirements.

2. Master Load Command

PETH, load WCP-MASTER. Read the actual job wording first. Identify the equipment, location, work method, energy sources, pressure, temperature, movement, access, entry, lifting, electrical, chemical, fire and environmental conditions that are stated or reasonably implied by the work. Do not invent a permit. Indicate the applicable permit, JSA, isolation, testing, PPE, evidence and close-out requirements supported by the job. Where information is missing, record a gap and require Department Head or Chief Engineer confirmation before work.

3. Core Principle

Job first. Hazard second. Control third. Permit only when triggered.

The packet must not assign every permit to every job. It shall identify the actual work features, connect them to the relevant hazards and controls, then indicate the likely work-control documents. Final applicability remains subject to the vessel SMS, permit matrix and responsible officer.

4. Work-Control Chain

Daily Work Plan → Risk Assessment → JSA where required → Permit to Work → Toolbox Talk → Crew Understanding → Work Execution → Close-Out → Weekly Submission
  • The Risk Assessment identifies hazards and controls.
  • The JSA, where required, converts the approved risk controls into the actual job sequence.
  • The PTW confirms that prerequisite controls are in place before work starts.
  • The TBT communicates the job-specific hazards, barriers, line-of-fire positions, responsibilities and stop-work triggers to the actual work team.
  • The Close-Out confirms restoration, leakage checks, guards, tools, personnel and final condition.

7. Higher-Risk JSA Decision

Assess JSA applicability when the work includes:

  • machinery overhaul;
  • air-compressor or purifier maintenance;
  • pressure-system or pipeline work;
  • enclosed-space entry;
  • work at height or overside;
  • hot work;
  • major cargo-system maintenance;
  • multiple isolations or interacting energy sources;
  • non-routine work, unfamiliar method or changed conditions.
Do not create unnecessary JSA paperwork for routine low-risk work. Record the reason for the decision. When JSA is required: generate it from the approved RA, brief the actual work team, resolve questions, obtain signatures and reassess if the scope or condition changes.

9. Line-of-Fire Doctrine

Line of fire means the path in which a person could be struck, sprayed, crushed, trapped, burned or injected by released, moving, falling, rotating, suspended, spring-loaded or pressurised energy.

Every applicable TBT shall answer:

  1. What can move, fall, rotate, swing, release or discharge?
  2. Where is the path of that energy?
  3. Where must the face, hands and body not be positioned?
  4. What exclusion zone or safe position is required?
  5. What tool will be used instead of hands?
  6. What condition requires immediate stop-work?
Required wording pattern: “Keep all personnel clear of the line of fire and the expected release or movement path. Do not place the face, hands or body in front of or between [identify component/path]. Stop the work if the energy cannot be isolated, the component cannot be secured, or any person enters the exclusion zone.”

10. High-Pressure and High-Temperature Rule

For hot or pressurised fuel, steam, hydraulic, pneumatic or process systems:

  1. Stop the equipment or transfer operation to an approved standby arrangement.
  2. Identify and isolate every source feeding the component.
  3. Apply LOTO.
  4. Allow the system and contained fluid to cool to a safe temperature.
  5. Drain and vent completely.
  6. Verify zero pressure and no continued discharge.
  7. Plan the first break and expected release path.
  8. Loosen gradually while standing outside the line of fire.
  9. Use suitable heat, splash and face protection.
  10. Restore pressure gradually and leak-check from a safe position.
Stop immediately if pressure remains, fluid continues to discharge, temperature is unsafe, isolation is doubtful or personnel cannot remain outside the release path.

Example — M.E. F.O. Fine Filter Change

Identified condition: Hot pressurised fuel-system maintenance.

Controls: Isolate inlet and outlet, apply LOTO, cool, drain, vent and verify zero pressure before opening. Keep the face and body away from the filter cover, vent, drain, flange and first-break connection. Loosen gradually from a protected position.

Likely documents: RA, TBT, pressure/pipeline PTW according to SMS, LOTO record and higher-risk JSA assessment.

Example — Removed D/G F.O. Fine Filter Cleaning

Identified condition: Residual fuel, possible retained pressure, spring-loaded parts, sharp edges, splash and fire risk.

Controls: Confirm removed from service, cold, drained and depressurised. Restrain the cover, stand clear of the line of fire, keep hands out of pinch points, use approved cleaning materials and protect the cleaned filter as a ready spare.

Permit decision: Do not automatically require a pressure permit when the filter is already removed, cold, drained and depressurised. Reassess if it remains connected, hot or pressurised.

11. Tank and Enclosed-Space Decision

Physical entry is the first decision.

No Personnel Entry

Where sludge, residue or inspection work is completed from the manhole using a long-handled scoop, cup, suction hose or other external method:

  • state “No personnel entry — work carried out externally”;
  • isolate connected systems;
  • control the opening;
  • maintain ventilation and communication;
  • keep the body outside the opening;
  • stop and reassess if external removal becomes unsafe or entry becomes necessary.

Personnel Entry

Enclosed-space permit, atmosphere testing, repeated gas readings, ventilation, attendant, communication, rescue readiness, entry/exit control and closure are required before entry.

12. Five-Sheet Work-Control Rules

  • Deck / Engine Toolbox Talk: Use only the vessel's approved hazard selections 1–18, the applicable expanded hazard wording, the approved Important selections and the approved Control Measures. Do not invent a separate hazard system.

13. Evidence Standard

Evidence may include:

  • signed TBT and JSA by the actual work team;
  • permit and supporting isolation records;
  • gas readings, pressure readings or zero-energy verification;
  • equipment condition, line-up or exclusion-zone photographs;
  • responsible-person confirmation;
  • completion and restoration records.
Photographs support the record but do not replace signatures, readings, permit details, verification or close-out.

14. Permit Validity and Record Integrity

  • Complete all testing, checks, signatures and approvals before the permit becomes valid.
  • Do not commence work with missing prerequisites.
  • Record changes in scope, conditions, personnel or hazards and reassess before continuing.
  • Do not backfill unsupported historical times, signatures, readings or initials.
  • Close the permit only after the system is restored and the final condition is verified.

15. Operational Coordination Rule

When cargo-hold cleaning, channeling, ballasting, maintenance and departure preparations overlap:

  • identify simultaneous operations and competing manpower;
  • protect critical departure and stability activities from interruption;
  • confirm communication between Deck and Engine Departments;
  • check ballast plan, tank levels, valve line-up, BWTS condition and port requirements;
  • avoid opening equipment or starting work that cannot be safely restored before manoeuvring or departure;
  • stop or defer non-critical work when the operational window becomes insufficient.

Do not create a false deficiency from the normal daily workflow.

22.6 Permit and Control Discipline

  • Do not maximise permits or controls to appear safer. Select only what the actual job and SMS trigger.
  • Do not describe a reviewed change as a reduction of safety. In the Chief Engineer review, state that permit, supervision and task-specific controls were checked and reinforced.
  • Removal of an unrelated control from a job does not remove supervision. Supervisor presence, briefing, PC/AB verification and stop-work authority shall be checked separately.
  • “Work on Lifts — SSF 014” means work on the shipboard lift system; it is not a general lifting-operation permit.
  • Pressure/pipeline, electrical, work-at-height, hot-work, enclosed-space, hydro-blasting, adverse-weather and SIMOPS selections require their actual trigger, not a keyword alone.

22.7 Toolbox Understanding and Visual Explanation

  • Supervisors are encouraged to use photographs, visual aids, diagrams, actual equipment and practical demonstrations when these improve crew understanding.
  • Where any crew member remains uncertain, the supervisor shall show the actual equipment or work area and demonstrate the correct method before work proceeds.
  • Images support understanding but do not replace RA, JSA, PTW, readings, signatures, supervisor verification or Close Out.

22.8 Close-Out Feedback Governance

  • During Close Out, supervisors are encouraged to obtain feedback from the crew involved through discussion of observations, difficulties, recommendations and shared experience.
  • Record only an additional hazard first identified during execution. Do not relabel an already planned hazard as a new finding.
  • Use verified feedback to strengthen future Work Planning, TBT, RA, JSA, PTW and control measures.
  • Do not invent a difficulty, recommendation, test result or satisfactory condition to fill an empty field.

22.9 BASSnet JSA Timing Rule

  • New JSA trial guidance applies from receipt onward and shall not be imposed retrospectively on records already prepared or executed.
  • Apply the trial at the next suitable selected higher-risk job, using the approved RA, pre-job discussion, actual work-team signatures and supporting PTW documentation.
  • Do not create additional JSA paperwork for routine low-risk work unless company guidance or the vessel SMS requires it.

24. WARD TBT Governance Rule

W.A.R.D. means: WATCH · ARMOR · REINFORCE · DEFEND.

Purpose: Convert the supplied WARD Vessel Doctrine into a disciplined pre-work, work-execution and intervention memory rule for Work Planning, RA/JSA/PTW alignment, Toolbox Talk delivery and Close Out.

Boundary: WARD does not replace the vessel SMS, approved risk assessment, JSA, Permit to Work, company checklist, Toolbox Talk form, responsible officer or Chief Engineer approval. It is a human-facing safety governance layer that must operate inside those controls.

WARD governing sequence:
WATCH → ARMOR → REINFORCE → DEFEND.
Observe the actual condition first. Protect the person before exposure. Secure the worksite and barriers before execution. Intervene immediately when a person, barrier or condition becomes unsafe.
W

WATCH

Maintain active situational awareness of the job, equipment, rigging, deck, access, atmosphere, weather, movement, pressure, temperature, people and surrounding operations. Identify what can change before and during the work.

A

ARMOR

Confirm the correct PPE and personal protective barriers before exposure. PPE shall match the actual hazard and shall never be treated as a substitute for isolation, ventilation, guarding, secure access or elimination of the hazard.

R

REINFORCE

Secure and verify the physical controls: isolations, LOTO, closures, lines, hatches, guards, tools, hoses, cargo, lifting gear, access, exclusion zones, communications and emergency arrangements. Recheck after breaks, changes or disturbance.

D

DEFEND

Use Stop Work authority and intervene immediately to protect any shipmate exposed to an unsafe act, failed barrier, changed condition or line-of-fire position. Work resumes only after the hazard is controlled, the team is re-briefed and authorization is restored.

24.2 Mandatory WARD Questions for Every Applicable TBT

  1. WATCH: What can move, fall, swing, release, energize, heat, pressurize, flood, ignite, expose or change during this job?
  2. ARMOR: What exact PPE and personal protection are required for those exposures, and has each item been checked before use?
  3. REINFORCE: Which isolations, barriers, closures, restraints, guards, access arrangements, communications and emergency controls must be secured and verified?
  4. DEFEND: What precise condition requires immediate intervention or Stop Work, who has authority to act, and what must be revalidated before restart?

24.3 WARD Non-Negotiable Rules

  • WARD shall be applied to the actual job, not copied as generic wording.
  • WATCH is continuous; it does not end after the Toolbox Talk.
  • ARMOR requires hazard-matched PPE, but PPE does not replace elimination, isolation or engineering controls.
  • REINFORCE means physically secure and verify; verbal assurance alone is insufficient for a critical barrier.
  • DEFEND includes intervention for another person. Rank, schedule, production pressure or familiarity shall not prevent Stop Work.
  • Changed scope, personnel, weather, atmosphere, energy state, access, equipment, SIMOPS or permit condition requires reassessment and re-briefing.
  • No WARD statement may be used to fabricate a check, reading, signature, time, approval, crew understanding or completed control.
WARD stop condition: Stop the work when observation is inadequate; required PPE is unavailable or defective; isolation, pressure, temperature, atmosphere, access, restraint, communication or emergency readiness is doubtful; a critical barrier is missing or disturbed; any person enters the line of fire; or the job no longer matches the approved plan, RA, JSA or permit.

24.4 WARD Restart Gate

Work may restart only after the unsafe condition is removed or controlled, affected permits and assessments remain valid or are reissued, critical barriers are reverified, the responsible officer approves continuation, and the actual work team is re-briefed on the changed condition.

24.5 WARD TBT Wording Pattern

WATCH the job and surrounding conditions continuously. ARMOR the team with the correct hazard-matched PPE before exposure. REINFORCE all isolations, restraints, closures, guards, access, exclusion zones and communication arrangements before starting and after any interruption. DEFEND every shipmate by intervening and stopping the work immediately when a barrier fails, conditions change, or any person enters the line of fire. Resume only after the controls are restored, verified and re-briefed.

24.6 Source Preservation and Interpretation Control

The exact supplied WARD text is preserved below as raw source evidence. The operational governance above corrects spacing and the apparent spelling error only in the interpreted display. It does not silently alter the archived source file.

25. WARD Major Permit Audit and Field-Completion Governance

Purpose: Require a field-by-field audit of every applicable Permit to Work and supporting checklist before the Chief Engineer review is finalized. This section was added after a manual vessel check identified that important enclosed-space entries had not been detected during the previous review.

Critical correction: A permit shall not be accepted merely because boxes are ticked or signatures are present. PETH must inspect all pages, handwritten notes, readings, frequencies, instrument details, validity periods, cross-references, entry and exit records, repeat-test records and closure fields. Where any part cannot be read or verified, PETH shall inform the Architect and request a clearer scan or reload. No invention, imagination, assumption, substitution or silent completion is permitted.

25.2 Gas-Testing Audit Rule

  • Calibration establishes instrument accuracy against a known standard at the required interval.
  • Bump test / functional test confirms that sensors, alarms, display and response operate when exposed to test gas before use.
  • Inspection confirms physical condition, battery, sampling hose/pump, filters, accessories and readiness.
  • These controls are related but not interchangeable. The audit shall verify each requirement that is applicable under the approved form, SMS and maker instructions.
  • Never invent a bump-test date, calibration date, instrument number, gas value, testing frequency, tester identity or time.

25.4 Two-Pass Permit Audit

  1. Pass 1 - Completeness and chronology: inspect every page and field; check identities, dates, times, readings, signatures, validity, cross-references, logs and closure. Do not rely on the first page or summary only.
  2. Pass 2 - Technical and safety alignment: compare the permit with the exact job, Work Plan, RA, JSA, TBT, equipment condition, operating status, maker requirements and applicable maritime/company controls.
  3. Record internal gaps and obtain correction or confirmation before issuing the positive office-facing review.

25.5 Image and Scan Quality Gate

Reload rule: If any page, handwriting, tick, numerical reading, gas name, date, time, signature, instrument detail or closure entry cannot be read confidently, PETH shall stop the audit and inform the Architect to reload a clearer scan or the original file. PETH shall not infer the missing content from surrounding entries or from another permit.

25.6 Office-Facing Review Release

The office-facing review shall remain positive, factual and human-facing only after the internal audit and correction gate are complete. It shall confirm the checks actually completed and shall not expose internal AI/Caberis scoring language. An unresolved material deficiency prevents final release; it is not converted into a false positive statement.

Governing sentence: Inspect every permit field, verify every life-safety reading and interval, correct before release, and never replace missing evidence with assumption.
